# Limitations and responsible use

This page is shown in the dashboard footer and in `markwright verify --help`.

**What a result means.** A Markwright result tells you how unusual a piece of text would be if it had *not*
been generated with a specific, registered watermark. "Strong evidence" means a human-written text of that
length would score this high very rarely, at the rate shown next to the result. It is a statistical
statement about one watermark configuration. It is not a statement about whether a person or a machine
wrote the text.

**What a result does not mean.**

- "No evidence" is not "written by a human." Most AI systems do not watermark at all; their text shows no
  evidence. So does watermarked text that has been paraphrased, translated, summarised or heavily edited.
- "Strong evidence" is not proof. At a false-positive target of 1 in 10,000, checking 10,000 ordinary human
  documents is expected to flag about one of them. The batch view shows the expected number.
- A positive can be forged. Anyone who learns a watermark key, or who has a published detector, can produce
  text that carries the mark. Such results are labelled "spoofing not excluded".

**Known ways marks are lost or weakened.** Paraphrasing; translation; shortening below the minimum detectable
length; low-entropy content (code boilerplate, lists, numbers, quotations); mixing marked and unmarked
passages; generation by open-weight copies of a model where the watermark was never applied. Every text
watermark can be removed by sufficient rewriting, and there is a ceiling on how detectable a mark can be
without changing the text's quality. Markwright reports published methods honestly; it cannot lift that ceiling.

**How to use results responsibly.** Treat a single result as one input among others, never as the sole basis
for a decision about a person. Read the false-positive rate and the token count every time. Say "consistent
with having been generated by model M with watermarking enabled", not "AI-generated". Export the signed
result and calibration report when a result will be relied on. Regulatory labelling duties concern how
providers mark outputs; a verification result is evidence about marking, not a legal determination.

**Privacy.** Markwright verifies text that people choose to submit, in deployments they control. It is not
designed, and should not be configured, to scan private communications, monitor employees' or students'
writing in bulk, or profile individuals. Submitted text is never stored.
